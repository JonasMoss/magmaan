\section*{Finding}

The near-nominal score-based Satorra--Bentler (SB) result in experiment 62
does not establish that mean scaling is generally correct. It is a finite-sample
bias--variance tradeoff. The tested population spectra are often close to
spherical, the one-degree-of-freedom cells make SB exact, and replacing the
estimated eigenvalues by their mean regularizes severe finite-sample spectral
noise. Where the mixture is harder, score-SB becomes liberal.

\section*{Reference laws}

Let $u=G^\top s$ be the efficient tested score, with $G$ obtained by
information-metric orthogonalization against the nuisance tangent. Define
\[
 A_G=G^\top A G,\qquad B_G=G^\top B G,\qquad
 T_A=u^\top A_G^{-1}u.
\]
Under the null,
\[
 T_A \Rightarrow \sum_{j=1}^q \lambda_j\chi^2_{1,j},
 \qquad
 \lambda_j=\operatorname{eig}(A_G^{-1}B_G).
\]
Score-SB uses
\[
 T_{\mathrm{SB}}=T_A/\bar\lambda,
 \qquad \bar\lambda=q^{-1}\sum_j\lambda_j,
\]
with a $\chi_q^2$ reference. The plug-in ALL test evaluates the weighted
chi-square tail using every estimated $\lambda_j$.

\begin{proposition}
Score-SB is exact for $q=1$ and when all population eigenvalues are equal. If
they differ, its reference variance is too small:
\[
 2q\bar\lambda^2 \leq 2\sum_j\lambda_j^2.
\]
Consequently, mean scaling generally understates upper-tail mass.
\end{proposition}

The direct sandwich score statistic instead uses
\[
 T_B=u^\top B_G^{-1}u \Rightarrow \chi_q^2.
\]
It is asymptotically pivotal when $B_G$ is consistently estimated and positive
definite. It avoids both a mixture-tail approximation and eigenvalue-by-eigenvalue
plug-in noise, although its inverse meat can itself be unstable at small sample
sizes.

\section*{Audit of experiment 62}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
Condition & Score-SB & Score-ALL \\
\midrule
Main grid, $q=1$ & 0.051 & 0.051 \\
Main grid, $q=4$ & 0.047 & 0.031 \\
Main grid, $q=8$ & 0.058 & 0.029 \\
Severe VM copula, $q=8$ & 0.078 & 0.025 \\
Severe PL copula, $q=8$ & 0.084 & 0.027 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The overall score-SB rejection rate of 0.052 averages over the exact scalar
case and hides the deterioration at eight restrictions. A diagnostic at
$q=8$, $N=200$ found median estimated eigenvalue coefficients of variation of
approximately 0.31 under normality, 0.69 under $t_5$, and 0.58 under skewness.
Large-sample diagnostics reduced these to approximately 0.06, 0.16, and 0.40.
Thus much of the finite-sample eigenvalue dispersion consumed by ALL is
estimation noise.

Using the large-sample diagnostic spectra as fixed mixture weights gave
theoretical SB rejection probabilities of approximately 0.050, 0.051, and
0.059 for normal, $t_5$, and skewed data. This reproduces the direction of the
observed calibration. SB is accurate where the tested spectrum is nearly
spherical and mildly liberal where it is not.

\begin{remark}
The good score-SB result is best interpreted as trace-only spectral shrinkage.
Its liberal mixture approximation offsets finite-sample conservatism from the
score base and noisy spectrum estimation. This cancellation is useful evidence
for regularization, not evidence that SB is the correct reference law.
\end{remark}

\section*{Direct-sandwich follow-up}

The implemented statistic $T_B$ was available for every successful main-grid
replication. Its null rejection was 0.036 overall, split as 0.051, 0.033, and
0.024 at one, four, and eight restrictions. At eight restrictions the median
condition number of $B_G$ was approximately 36, 22, and 14 at total
$N=60,100,200$. Thus direct inversion is more conservative precisely where the
estimated spectrum is noisiest.

Under the severe eight-df copulas, direct-sandwich rejection was 0.029 for the
piecewise-linear generator and 0.067 for Vale--Maurelli. The corresponding
average cellwise condition numbers were approximately 251 and 96. Asymptotic
pivotality therefore does not supply uniform finite-sample calibration when the
efficient meat is ill-conditioned.

After matched empirical-null calibration, direct-sandwich power was 0.072 and
0.120 for dense effects 0.25 and 0.50, and 0.126 and 0.302 for sparse effects.
This is somewhat below the spectrum-score methods, so the additional nominal
conservatism does not reveal a compensating power advantage.

In the 32-cell dimension replay, direct-sandwich rejection ranged from 0.038 to
0.061 after averaging over copula and geometry. At the adequate sample ratio it
was 0.050, 0.046, 0.043, and 0.058 for $p=10,20,30,40$. Mean median condition
numbers fell from about 45 to 22 over the same sequence because total $N$ grew
with dimension. This supports asymptotic recovery with information, while also
showing that ambient dimension alone is not the conditioning driver.

The resulting provisional ordering is not SB versus sandwich. It is a
regularization continuum: SB collapses the spectrum to its trace, pEBA shrinks
its shape, ALL uses the noisy shape directly, and direct sandwich inverts the
noisy meat. In experiment 62, pEBA4 is the most stable compromise.

\section*{Iteration consequences}

The next experiment revision must report the following separately:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item nominal size by restriction dimension, rather than only an overall
        average;
  \item score-SB, score-ALL, pEBA, standardized flips, and the direct sandwich
        score statistic;
  \item eigenvalue mean, coefficient of variation, and condition diagnostics;
  \item availability and conditioning of $B_G^{-1}$;
  \item matched-null power, explicitly labeled as a comparison after forced
        size alignment rather than evidence of nominal validity.
\end{enumerate}

The report should avoid the generic claim that the score base makes SB correct.
A defensible provisional conclusion is narrower: in these designs the score
base is less liberal than the likelihood-ratio base, while trace shrinkage and
pEBA can outperform a noisy plug-in spectrum at small sample sizes. The direct
sandwich comparison determines whether that advantage survives without an SB
mixture approximation.
