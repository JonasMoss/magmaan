\section*{Purpose}

The Guttman estimator used by \texttt{magmaan} is best understood as a
covariance-summary map, not as the minimizer of the usual CFA covariance
discrepancy. It does contain a clean minimization step, but that step is a
regression of observed variables on fixed indicator composites. The CFA
parameters are then read from that regression after choosing a latent scale.

This note records the construction in enough detail to audit an implementation.
It also states which discrepancy is natural for multi-sample constraints. The
answer is:
\begin{itemize}
\item the construction itself minimizes a composite-score prediction error;
\item NTML and ULS discrepancies evaluated at the Guttman estimate are valid
  residual statistics, but not native likelihood-ratio statistics;
\item the native constrained or multi-sample test is a minimum-distance or Wald
  statistic in the estimator's own asymptotic covariance metric.
\end{itemize}

Here ``Guttman estimator'' means Guttman's historical multiple-group method for
common-factor analysis \cite{guttman1952}. The historical phrase
``multiple-group'' names groups of indicators used to form composites. It is not
multi-group SEM. Multi-group SEM enters later by stacking independent sample
blocks.

\section{Single-block setup}

Fix one continuous CFA block with \(p\) observed variables and \(k\) factors.
Let \(S\) be the sample covariance and let
\[
  Z\in\{0,1\}^{p\times k}
\]
be the indicator-by-factor incidence matrix. In the simple-structure case used
by the current estimator, each row of \(Z\) has exactly one nonzero entry:
\(Z_{if}=1\) means that observed variable \(i\) is assigned to factor \(f\).
Write \(f(i)\) for that assigned factor, and let \(m_f\) denote the marker
indicator for factor \(f\).

The estimator is defined only at regular points. Each factor must have at least
three indicators, the within-factor correlations used below must avoid zero
denominators, the composite covariance must be positive definite, and the
marker coefficient produced by the composite regression must be nonzero.

\section{Step 1: replace variances by communalities}

The first step creates a covariance-like matrix \(H\) whose off-diagonal entries
are the observed covariances and whose diagonal entries are estimated common
variances. Write \(R\) for the correlation matrix of \(S\). For an indicator
\(i\), let
\[
  P_i
  =
  \{(j,\ell): j<\ell,\ j\ne i,\ \ell\ne i,\ f(j)=f(\ell)=f(i)\}.
\]
The Spearman-Harman correlation-scale communality is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spearman-h}
  \hat h_i^{\mathrm{cor}}
  =
  |P_i|^{-1}
  \sum_{(j,\ell)\in P_i}
    \frac{R_{ij}R_{i\ell}}{R_{j\ell}} .
\end{equation}
Convert it back to covariance scale by
\[
  \hat h_i = S_{ii}\hat h_i^{\mathrm{cor}},
  \qquad
  \hat u_i = S_{ii}-\hat h_i ,
\]
and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H}
  H
  =
  S-\diag(S)+\diag(\hat h)
  =
  S-\diag(\hat u).
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Exact recovery of the diagonal common variance]
\label{lem:spearman}
Suppose \(i,j,\ell\) are indicators of the same one-dimensional factor, with
population covariances
\[
  \Sigma_{ab}=\lambda_a\lambda_b\phi
  \qquad (a\ne b).
\]
Then every ratio in \eqref{eq:spearman-h} equals
\[
  \lambda_i^2\phi / \Sigma_{ii}
\]
on the correlation scale, and therefore \(h_i=\lambda_i^2\phi\) on the
covariance scale.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On the covariance scale,
\[
  \frac{\Sigma_{ij}\Sigma_{i\ell}}{\Sigma_{j\ell}}
  =
  \frac{(\lambda_i\lambda_j\phi)(\lambda_i\lambda_\ell\phi)}
       {\lambda_j\lambda_\ell\phi}
  =
  \lambda_i^2\phi .
\]
Dividing each covariance by the relevant standard deviations gives the
correlation-scale identity and multiplying back by \(\Sigma_{ii}\) returns the
same covariance-scale common variance.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The smooth asymptotic notes treat the interior map, where no numerical repair is
active. The shared \texttt{theta\_spearman()} helper used by the current C++
code clips the correlation-scale communality to wide bounds before converting
to a residual variance. The frontier estimator rejects factors with fewer than
three indicators before the helper's small-block fallback can matter. If a
bound is active, the map is piecewise smooth rather than smooth at that point,
and the finite-difference Jacobian is differentiating that active branch.
\end{remark}

\section{Step 2: regress variables on fixed composites}

This step is a population regression problem. Let \(Y_H\) be a centered
auxiliary \(p\)-vector with covariance
\[
  \Cov(Y_H)=H .
\]
The subscript is only a reminder: \(Y_H\) is not new observed data, but a
population device for writing the least-squares problem whose second moments
are the communality-filled matrix \(H\). If \(H\) is not positive semidefinite,
the same formulas still define a quadratic algebraic map, but the literal
random-vector interpretation is lost.

The fixed indicator composites are
\[
  U=Z\T Y_H .
\]
Their covariance is \(Z\T HZ\), and their covariance with the variables is
\(HZ\):
\[
  \Cov(U)=Z\T H Z,
  \qquad
  \Cov(Y_H,U)=HZ .
\]

For fixed \(U\), choose a coefficient matrix \(K\in\mathbb R^{p\times k}\) to
minimize the population prediction error
\begin{equation}\label{eq:composite-loss}
  K^\star
  =
  \argmin_{K\in\mathbb R^{p\times k}}
  \E_H\{ \lVert Y_H-KU\rVert^2 \}.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the objective is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:composite-objective}
  Q_{\mathrm{comp}}(K)
  =
  \tr(H)-2\tr(KZ\T H)+\tr\{K(Z\T HZ)K\T\}.
\end{equation}
The subscript \(H\) emphasizes that the expectation uses only the
communality-filled second moments. Row \(i\) of \(K\) is the population least
squares coefficient vector in the regression of \(Y_{H,i}\) on the fixed
composites \(U\). The stacked problem in \eqref{eq:composite-loss} is just
the sum of those \(p\) regressions.

\begin{lemma}[Composite regression step]\label{lem:regression}
If \(Z\T HZ\) is positive definite, the unique minimizer of
\eqref{eq:composite-loss} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Kstar}
  K^\star = HZ(Z\T HZ)^{-1}.
\end{equation}
The common covariance reproduced by the composites is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:common-proj}
  K^\star (Z\T HZ) K^{\star\T}
  =
  H Z (Z\T H Z)^{-1} Z\T H .
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate \eqref{eq:composite-objective} with respect to \(K\):
\[
  \frac{\partial Q_{\mathrm{comp}}}{\partial K}
  =
  -2HZ+2K(Z\T HZ) .
\]
The first-order condition is \(K(Z\T HZ)=HZ\), giving
\(K^\star=HZ(Z\T HZ)^{-1}\). Since \(Z\T HZ\) is positive definite, this
stationary point is the unique minimum. Substitution gives
\eqref{eq:common-proj}.
\end{proof}

This is the actual least-squares part of the Guttman estimator. The variables
being optimized are the coefficients from fixed indicator composites to the
observed variables. The CFA covariance model parameters have not yet entered as
optimization variables.

\section{Step 3: choose a latent scale for the common part}

The regression step already gives a rank-\(k\) common covariance:
\[
  \widehat H_{\mathrm{com}}
  =
  K^\star (Z\T HZ) K^{\star\T}.
\]
This can be read as a factor model in the composite coordinate system. If the
surrogate factor vector is just \(U\), then its covariance is \(Z\T HZ\), its
loadings are \(K^\star\), and
\[
  K^\star U
\]
is the fitted common part of \(Y_H\). At this point there has been no attempt
to put the factors in the usual CFA scale. The columns are still measured in
the units of the indicator composites.

More generally, any nonsingular coordinate transform \(C\) gives the same
common covariance. Define
\[
  \eta_C=C U,\qquad
  \Lambda_C=K^\star C^{-1},\qquad
  \Phi_C=C(Z\T HZ)C\T .
\]
Then \(K^\star U=\Lambda_C\eta_C\), \(\Cov(\eta_C)=\Phi_C\), and
\[
  \Lambda_C\Phi_C\Lambda_C\T
  =
  K^\star (Z\T HZ) K^{\star\T}.
\]
Thus the \((\Lambda,\Phi)\) reconstruction is not a second optimization
problem. It is a choice of coordinates for the common covariance already fitted
by the composite regression. The incidence matrix \(Z\) gives labelled
composite axes, so the usual Guttman choices do not use an arbitrary rotation
\(C\). They keep those axes and only change the scale of each column.

The unit-variance scale is the tidiest diagonal choice. Let
\[
  C_{\mathrm{uv}}
  =
  \diag\{(Z\T HZ)_{11}^{-1/2},\ldots,(Z\T HZ)_{kk}^{-1/2}\},
\]
assuming the composite variances are positive, and define
\[
  \eta_{\mathrm{uv}}=C_{\mathrm{uv}}U .
\]
Then \(U=C_{\mathrm{uv}}^{-1}\eta_{\mathrm{uv}}\), so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unit-variance-map}
  \Lambda_{\mathrm{uv}}=K^\star C_{\mathrm{uv}}^{-1},
  \qquad
  \Phi_{\mathrm{uv}}=C_{\mathrm{uv}}(Z\T HZ)C_{\mathrm{uv}},
  \qquad
  \psi_{\mathrm{uv}}
  =
  \diag\{S-\Lambda_{\mathrm{uv}}\Phi_{\mathrm{uv}}
  \Lambda_{\mathrm{uv}}\T\}.
\end{equation}
By construction, \(\diag(\Phi_{\mathrm{uv}})=I\), and the common covariance is
unchanged:
\[
  \Lambda_{\mathrm{uv}}\Phi_{\mathrm{uv}}\Lambda_{\mathrm{uv}}\T
  =
  K^\star (Z\T HZ) K^{\star\T}.
\]
The entries of \(\Phi_{\mathrm{uv}}\) are the correlations among the fixed
indicator composites under \(H\), and the loadings are the regression
coefficients expressed in those unit-variance latent coordinates.

The first-loading-equals-one scale is another diagonal reparameterization. Let
\[
  M=\diag(K^\star_{m_1,1},\ldots,K^\star_{m_k,k})
\]
be the diagonal matrix of the marker coefficients in the raw composite
coordinates. Equivalently, define the first-loading-scaled surrogate factor
vector
\[
  \eta_G=M U .
\]
Then \(U=M^{-1}\eta_G\), so the first-loading-scaled Guttman matrices are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:marker-map}
  \Lambda_G=K^\star M^{-1},
  \qquad
  \Phi_G=M(Z\T HZ)M,
  \qquad
  \psi_G=\diag\{S-\Lambda_G\Phi_G\Lambda_G\T\}.
\end{equation}
Every marker loading in \(\Lambda_G\) is one, and the common covariance is
unchanged:
\[
  \Lambda_G\Phi_G\Lambda_G\T
  =
  K^\star (Z\T HZ) K^{\star\T}.
\]
Among diagonal rescalings that preserve the factor labels supplied by \(Z\),
this is the unique rescaling that makes the chosen marker loadings equal to
one, provided all marker coefficients are nonzero.

The two displays are the same estimator written in different latent scales.
Starting from the unit-variance matrices, define
\[
  M_{\mathrm{uv}}
  =
  \diag\{(\Lambda_{\mathrm{uv}})_{m_1,1},\ldots,
          (\Lambda_{\mathrm{uv}})_{m_k,k}\}.
\]
Then
\[
  \Lambda_G=\Lambda_{\mathrm{uv}}M_{\mathrm{uv}}^{-1},
  \qquad
  \Phi_G=M_{\mathrm{uv}}\Phi_{\mathrm{uv}}M_{\mathrm{uv}} .
\]
This conversion is estimator-independent. It is the usual change of latent
factor units from unit variances to first-loading-equals-one units.

In the current \texttt{magmaan} map, the full \(K^\star\) is computed. The
reported free loading parameters are read only from the model-allowed loading
cells. Off-pattern entries of \(K^\star\) are auxiliary. They vanish on the
exact simple-structure model; away from that model they are not fitted as
cross-loadings. The residual variances in \eqref{eq:unit-variance-map} and
\eqref{eq:marker-map} are identical because they come from the diagonal of the
same composite common part.

\begin{proposition}[Exact recovery on the simple CFA model]\label{prop:recover}
Suppose the communality-filled population matrix is exactly
\[
  H_0=\Lambda_0\Phi_0\Lambda_0\T ,
\]
where \(\Lambda_0\) has the simple loading pattern encoded by \(Z\), each marker
loading is one, and the diagonal matrix \(T=\Lambda_0\T Z\) is nonsingular.
Then the first-loading-scaled Guttman map recovers \(\Lambda_0\) and
\(\Phi_0\). The unit-variance map gives the corresponding unit-variance
parameterization of the same common covariance.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Because every indicator loads on one factor, \(T=\Lambda_0\T Z\) is diagonal.
Thus
\[
  H_0Z=\Lambda_0\Phi_0T,
  \qquad
  Z\T H_0Z=T\Phi_0T .
\]
The raw composite-regression coefficients are
\[
  H_0Z(Z\T H_0Z)^{-1}
  =
  \Lambda_0\Phi_0T(T\Phi_0T)^{-1}
  =
  \Lambda_0T^{-1}.
\]
The marker coefficient in column \(f\) is therefore \(1/T_{ff}\), since the
marker loading is one. Dividing the column by this marker coefficient gives
\(\Lambda_0\). With \(M=T^{-1}\),
\[
  M(Z\T H_0Z)M=\Phi_0 .
\]
The unit-variance display \eqref{eq:unit-variance-map} has the same common
covariance and \(\diag(\Phi_{\mathrm{uv}})=I\). It is therefore the
unit-variance reparameterization of the recovered first-loading-scale model, up
to the arbitrary signs of the latent axes.
\end{proof}

\section{What is and is not minimized}

The preceding lemmas give the clean answer. The Guttman construction minimizes
the composite prediction loss \eqref{eq:composite-objective}. It does not generally
minimize an SEM covariance discrepancy
\[
  F_V(\theta)
  =
  \{\vech(S)-\sigma(\theta)\}\T
  V
  \{\vech(S)-\sigma(\theta)\},
\]
where \(V\) is the ULS, GLS, WLS, or NTML weight. A minimizer of \(F_V\) has the
first-order condition
\[
  \Delta(\theta)\T V
  \{\vech(S)-\sigma(\theta)\}=0,
\]
or the equivalent NTML score condition when \(V\) is the likelihood weight. The
Guttman estimate is obtained from \(H\), \(Z\), and the latent-scale choice
above; there is no reason for it to satisfy that residual orthogonality
condition except in special cases.

Thus NTML and ULS are fine as \emph{post-estimation residual statistics}. They
are not the discrepancies that define the point estimate.

\section{Boundary-complete extension}

Equations \eqref{eq:Kstar} and \eqref{eq:common-proj} use the inverse of the
composite covariance \(Z\T HZ\), so the interior construction is not
automatically total. A boundary-complete version should distinguish three
cases.

First, if \(Z\T HZ\) is positive definite, use the ordinary Guttman map above.
Second, if \(Z\T HZ\) is positive semidefinite and singular, use the
Moore-Penrose inverse when the covariance equations are compatible:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compat}
  HZ=HZ(Z\T HZ)^{+}(Z\T HZ) .
\end{equation}
Then
\[
  K_{\mathrm{MP}}=HZ(Z\T HZ)^{+}
\]
is the unique minimum-Frobenius-norm minimizer of
\eqref{eq:composite-objective}. It is also the ridge limit
\[
  \lim_{\lambda\downarrow0}HZ(Z\T HZ+\lambda I)^{-1}.
\]
All minimizers give the same fitted common covariance,
\[
  K(Z\T HZ)K\T=HZ(Z\T HZ)^{+}Z\T H ,
\]
so the common covariance is identified even though the coordinate
representation of the factors is not.

The parameter split is the difficult part. A singular \(Z\T HZ\) means that
the fixed composite space has rank below \(k\). The common covariance still has
a canonical Moore-Penrose fitted value, but the marker-scaled
\((\Lambda_G,\Phi_G)\) representative is a convention. Marker rescaling can
also fail if \(K_{\mathrm{MP},m_f f}=0\) for some marker. A boundary-complete
implementation should therefore return the common covariance and the
Moore-Penrose representative with an explicit boundary warning, and should not
present the individual factor parameters as regularly identified.

Third, if \(Z\T HZ\) is indefinite or \eqref{eq:compat} fails, the quadratic
\eqref{eq:composite-objective} has no pure Guttman least-squares solution. An
always-returning estimator can still be defined by a named regularized mode,
for example
\[
  K_{\lambda}=HZ(Z\T HZ+\lambda I)^{-1},
\]
with \(\lambda\) chosen to make the system positive definite, or by first
projecting \(Z\T HZ\) to a positive semidefinite matrix. This is useful
engineering, but it should be reported as a regularized Guttman variant, not as
the clean population map.

\section{Residual statistics at the Guttman estimate}

Let \(s=\vech(S)\), let \(\hat\theta_G=\tau_G(s)\), and let
\[
  r=s-\sigma(\hat\theta_G).
\]
For a fixed or model-implied weight \(V\), define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:TV}
  T_V=N r\T V r .
\end{equation}
This includes ULS with the vech metric and the NTML residual quadratic form with
the model-implied normal-theory weight evaluated at
\(\Sigma(\hat\theta_G)\).

Let
\[
  J_G=\frac{\partial\tau_G}{\partial s\T},
  \qquad
  \Delta=\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial\theta\T}
\]
at the target point. Under exact fit, Fisher consistency gives
\[
  J_G\Delta=I .
\]
Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:resid-projector}
  \sqrt N\,r
  =
  M_G\,\sqrt N(s-\sigma_0)+o_p(1),
  \qquad
  M_G=I-\Delta J_G .
\end{equation}
The matrix \(M_G\) is a residual projector, but it is generally an oblique
projector. It is not the \(V\)-orthogonal residual projector of the
\(F_V\)-minimizer.

Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixture}
  T_V
  \Rightarrow
  \sum_j \lambda_j \chi^2_{1,j},
\end{equation}
where the nonzero \(\lambda_j\)'s are the eigenvalues of
\[
  (M_G\T V M_G)\Gamma .
\]
Only when \(J_G\) equals the \(V\)-least-squares Jacobian and \(V\) is the
matching inverse moment covariance does this collapse to an ordinary
\(\chi^2_{df}\). That is the efficient ML or GLS special case, not the Guttman
case.

For nested models, the LRT-shaped difference
\[
  T_d=T_{V,0}-T_{V,1}
\]
is therefore a pseudo-LRT. Its reference is obtained from the difference of the
two quadratic forms. Since neither fitted value is required to minimize
\(F_V\), \(T_d\) is not guaranteed to be nonnegative in finite samples.

\section{Multi-sample blocks and the native constrained test}

For independent sample groups \(g=1,\ldots,G\), apply the same Guttman map to
each covariance block:
\[
  \hat\theta_g=\tau_G(s_g),
  \qquad
  J_g=\frac{\partial\tau_G}{\partial s_g\T}.
\]
Stack \(\hat\theta=(\hat\theta_1\T,\ldots,\hat\theta_G\T)\T\). If
\(\Gamma_g\) is the fourth-moment covariance of \(s_g\), then the finite-sample
delta covariance of the stacked estimator is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Omega-block}
  \Omega
  =
  \bigoplus_{g=1}^G
  \frac{1}{N_g}J_g\Gamma_gJ_g\T .
\end{equation}
The configural Guttman estimator imposes no cross-block equality constraints.
It simply estimates every block independently and stacks the results.

Now impose a linear invariance constraint
\[
  R\theta=c
\]
on the stacked parameter vector. The natural constrained Guttman estimator is
the minimum-distance projection
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projection}
  \tilde\theta
  =
  \hat\theta
  -
  \Omega R\T(R\Omega R\T)^{-1}(R\hat\theta-c).
\end{equation}
It is the solution of
\[
  \tilde\theta
  =
  \argmin_{\eta:\ R\eta=c}
  (\hat\theta-\eta)\T\Omega^{+}(\hat\theta-\eta),
\]
with the usual interpretation on the estimable constraint subspace.

The associated statistic is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wald}
  W
  =
  (R\hat\theta-c)\T
  (R\Omega R\T)^{-1}
  (R\hat\theta-c).
\end{equation}
Under the null, \(W\Rightarrow\chi^2_{\rank(R)}\). This is the native
Guttman discrepancy for invariance work: distance in the estimator's own
asymptotic parameter metric. It is monotone, closed form, and does not require
pretending that the point estimate minimized NTML or ULS.

\section{Audit checklist}

For one covariance block, the implementation can be checked in this order:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Build the incidence matrix \(Z\) from the simple CFA loading pattern.
\item For each factor block, compute the Spearman-Harman communalities
  \eqref{eq:spearman-h}; place them on the diagonal to get \(H\).
\item Compute the variable-composite covariance \(HZ\) and the composite
  covariance \(Z\T HZ\).
\item Solve \(K^\star=HZ(Z\T HZ)^{-1}\). This is the coefficient matrix
  minimizing the composite prediction loss when \(Z\T HZ\) is positive
  definite.
\item Choose a latent scale. For unit variances, form
  \(C_{\mathrm{uv}}\) from the inverse square roots of the diagonal entries of
  \(Z\T HZ\), then compute
  \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{uv}}=K^\star C_{\mathrm{uv}}^{-1}\),
  \(\Phi_{\mathrm{uv}}=C_{\mathrm{uv}}(Z\T HZ)C_{\mathrm{uv}}\), and
  \(\psi_{\mathrm{uv}}=\diag(S-\Lambda_{\mathrm{uv}}\Phi_{\mathrm{uv}}
  \Lambda_{\mathrm{uv}}\T)\).
\item If first-loading-equals-one output is desired, convert the
  unit-variance matrices by the marker loadings or compute
  \(\Lambda_G=K^\star M^{-1}\), \(\Phi_G=M(Z\T HZ)M\), and
  \(\psi_G=\diag(S-\Lambda_G\Phi_G\Lambda_G\T)\) directly.
\item Assemble the model's free parameter vector from the allowed loading cells,
  latent covariance cells, and residual variances. Off-pattern entries of
  \(K^\star\) are auxiliary unless the model itself allows those loadings.
\item For standard errors or tests, finite-difference the full map to obtain
  \(J_G\), then use \(J_G\Gamma J_G\T/N\) for parameter covariance and
  \(M_G=I-\Delta J_G\) for residual goodness-of-fit references.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Summary}

The Guttman estimator has a real least-squares criterion, but it is not the SEM
criterion used by ML, ULS, GLS, or WLS. It first constructs fixed indicator
composites, regresses the observed variables on those composites under the
communality-filled covariance \(H\), and then chooses a latent scale for the
resulting common covariance. Unit-variance and first-loading-equals-one output
are diagonal reparameterizations of the same fitted common part. Residual NTML
or ULS statistics are useful diagnostics and valid pseudo-tests once their
weighted-\(\chi^2\) reference is corrected with
\(M_G=I-\Delta J_G\). For multi-sample invariance, the more natural test is the
minimum-distance/Wald statistic based on the block-diagonal Guttman delta
covariance.
