\section*{Purpose}

Dhaene and Rosseel \cite{dhaene2024} define their multiple-group estimator by
replacing the diagonal of the sample covariance matrix with estimated
communalities. They use Spearman's rule as described by Harman
\cite{harman1976}, and note that other communality or uniqueness estimators
exist, including the uniqueness estimator of Ihara and Kano \cite{iharakano1986}
and Cudeck \cite{cudeck1991}. This note records the population requirement a
communality rule must satisfy before it can be used as part of the Guttman
estimator rather than merely as a start-value heuristic.

The required target is the diagonal of the common covariance,
\[
  c_i(\Sigma_0)
  =
  e_i\T \Lambda\Phi\Lambda\T e_i,
\]
not an arbitrary lower bound, reliability coefficient, or squared multiple
correlation. In a simple-structure CFA block, where indicator \(i\) loads only
on factor \(f(i)\),
\[
  c_i=\lambda_i^2\Phi_{f(i)f(i)}.
\]

\section{Population setup}

Consider one factor block \(I_f\) with at least three indicators and diagonal
residual covariance within the block:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-model}
  y_i=\lambda_i\eta_f+\epsilon_i,\qquad
  \Var(\eta_f)=\phi_f,\qquad
  \Cov(\epsilon_i,\epsilon_j)=0\quad(i\ne j).
\end{equation}
For \(i\ne j\) in the same block,
\[
  \sigma_{ij}=\lambda_i\lambda_j\phi_f.
\]
The common diagonal entry for item \(i\) is
\[
  c_i=\lambda_i^2\phi_f,
  \qquad
  \theta_i=\sigma_{ii}-c_i.
\]
The Guttman matrix \(H\) is obtained by replacing \(\sigma_{ii}\) by \(c_i\), or
equivalently by subtracting \(\theta_i\) from the diagonal.

\begin{remark}
For a multi-factor simple CFA, the same within-factor formulas hold factor by
factor. Cross-factor covariances involve \(\Phi_{fg}\) and should not be used in
the communality triads below. The target remains
\(\diag(\Lambda\Phi\Lambda\T)\).
\end{remark}

\section{Spearman triad identities}

\begin{proposition}[Single triad]\label{prop:triad}
Let \(i,j,k\in I_f\) be distinct indicators with
\(\lambda_j\lambda_k\phi_f\ne0\). Under \eqref{eq:block-model},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:single-triad}
  h_{i;jk}(\Sigma)
  =
  \frac{\sigma_{ij}\sigma_{ik}}{\sigma_{jk}}
  =
  c_i .
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Substitute the within-factor covariances:
\[
  \frac{\sigma_{ij}\sigma_{ik}}{\sigma_{jk}}
  =
  \frac{(\lambda_i\lambda_j\phi_f)(\lambda_i\lambda_k\phi_f)}
       {\lambda_j\lambda_k\phi_f}
  =
  \lambda_i^2\phi_f
  =
  c_i .
\]
\end{proof}

Thus every admissible pair \((j,k)\) gives a population-consistent estimator of
the same diagonal common variance. Pooling pairs changes finite-sample behavior,
not the population target.

\section{Two consistent Spearman poolings}

Let
\[
  \mathcal P_i=\{(j,k): j<k,\ j,k\in I_{f(i)},\ j\ne i,\ k\ne i\}.
\]
Assume all required denominators are nonzero.

\paragraph{Average of ratios.}
The lavaan helper used by \texttt{lav\_cfa\_theta\_spearman()} and the current
\texttt{magmaan} helper averages the triad ratios on the correlation scale. On
the covariance scale the unclamped population functional is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:avg-ratio}
  h_i^{\mathrm{AR}}(\Sigma)
  =
  \frac{1}{|\mathcal P_i|}
  \sum_{(j,k)\in\mathcal P_i}
  \frac{\sigma_{ij}\sigma_{ik}}{\sigma_{jk}} .
\end{equation}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:triad}, \(h_i^{\mathrm{AR}}(\Sigma_0)=c_i\).

\paragraph{Ratio of sums.}
Dhaene and Rosseel's printed formula is the Harman/Spearman ratio-of-sums
pooling:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ratio-sums}
  h_i^{\mathrm{RS}}(\Sigma)
  =
  \frac{\sum_{(j,k)\in\mathcal P_i}\sigma_{ij}\sigma_{ik}}
       {\sum_{(j,k)\in\mathcal P_i}\sigma_{jk}} .
\end{equation}
Under \eqref{eq:block-model},
\[
  \sum_{(j,k)}\sigma_{ij}\sigma_{ik}
  =
  \lambda_i^2\phi_f
  \sum_{(j,k)}\sigma_{jk},
\]
so \(h_i^{\mathrm{RS}}(\Sigma_0)=c_i\) whenever the denominator does not vanish.

\begin{remark}
The two poolings are equivalent on the exact model but differ off model and in
finite samples. The ratio of sums is often numerically calmer when individual
\(\sigma_{jk}\)'s are small. The average of ratios avoids cancellation in the
pooled denominator but can be sensitive to a single weak pair. Both are genuine
population-consistent communality rules under the simple block model.
\end{remark}

\section{Linear method-of-moments triad family}

The ratio identity can be written without ratios. Work on the correlation
scale within one factor block. Under \eqref{eq:block-model},
\[
  \rho_{ij}=\alpha_i\alpha_j\qquad(i\ne j),
\]
where
\[
  \alpha_i=\lambda_i\sqrt{\phi_f/\sigma_{ii}} .
\]
The correlation-scale communality proportion is
\[
  \gamma_i=\alpha_i^2,
  \qquad
  c_i=\sigma_{ii}\gamma_i .
\]
For each pair \((j,k)\in\mathcal P_i\), the Spearman identity is equivalent to
the linear moment
\begin{equation}\label{eq:linear-triad-moment}
  \gamma_i\rho_{jk}-\rho_{ij}\rho_{ik}=0 .
\end{equation}
Thus Spearman's rule is not an ad hoc population identity. It is one way of
pooling exact rank-one triad moments. The estimator-design question is which
finite-sample pooling of \eqref{eq:linear-triad-moment} to use.

For a sample correlation matrix \(R\), define for item \(i\)
\[
  a_{i,jk}=R_{jk},
  \qquad
  b_{i,jk}=R_{ij}R_{ik},
  \qquad
  (j,k)\in\mathcal P_i .
\]
Stack these into vectors \(a_i\) and \(b_i\). For any symmetric positive
semidefinite weight \(W_i\) satisfying \(a_i\T W_i a_i>0\), define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:item-linear-mom}
  \hat\gamma_i(W_i)
  =
  (a_i\T W_i a_i)^{-1}a_i\T W_i b_i,
  \qquad
  \hat c_i(W_i)=S_{ii}\hat\gamma_i(W_i).
\end{equation}
This is the closed-form weighted least-squares estimator of \(\gamma_i\) in
the linear moments
\[
  a_i\gamma_i-b_i=0 .
\]

\begin{proposition}[Linear triad moments are exact]\label{prop:linear-mom}
Let \(\Sigma_0\) satisfy the one-factor block model
\eqref{eq:block-model}. Let \(R_0\) be its correlation matrix, and suppose
\(W_i(R_0)\) is finite with
\[
  a_i(R_0)\T W_i(R_0)a_i(R_0)>0 .
\]
Then the population version of \eqref{eq:item-linear-mom} satisfies
\[
  \gamma_i\{W_i(R_0)\}=\alpha_i^2,
  \qquad
  c_i\{W_i(R_0)\}=\lambda_i^2\phi_f .
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At the population model,
\[
  b_{i,jk}(R_0)
  =
  \rho_{ij}\rho_{ik}
  =
  \alpha_i^2\rho_{jk}
  =
  \gamma_i a_{i,jk}(R_0)
\]
for every \((j,k)\in\mathcal P_i\). Hence \(b_i(R_0)=a_i(R_0)\gamma_i\), and
\[
  (a_i\T W_i a_i)^{-1}a_i\T W_i b_i
  =
  (a_i\T W_i a_i)^{-1}a_i\T W_i a_i\,\gamma_i
  =
  \gamma_i .
\]
Multiplying by \(\sigma_{ii}\) returns \(c_i=\lambda_i^2\phi_f\).
\end{proof}

Several familiar and useful rules are special cases.

\paragraph{Identity linear moments.}
With \(W_i=I\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:identity-linear-mom}
  \hat\gamma_i^{\mathrm{ILM}}
  =
  \frac{\sum_{(j,k)\in\mathcal P_i}R_{jk}R_{ij}R_{ik}}
       {\sum_{(j,k)\in\mathcal P_i}R_{jk}^2}.
\end{equation}
This is the simplest closed-form method-of-moments version. It avoids division
by each individual \(R_{jk}\), so it is less sensitive than the average of
ratios to a single weak denominator.

\paragraph{Average-of-ratios Spearman.}
If all denominators are nonzero, the average-of-ratios rule
\eqref{eq:avg-ratio} is obtained from \eqref{eq:item-linear-mom} by the
data-dependent weight
\[
  W_i=\diag\{R_{jk}^{-2}:(j,k)\in\mathcal P_i\}.
\]
Then \(a_i\T W_i a_i=|\mathcal P_i|\) and
\[
  \hat\gamma_i(W_i)
  =
  |\mathcal P_i|^{-1}
  \sum_{(j,k)\in\mathcal P_i}
  \frac{R_{ij}R_{ik}}{R_{jk}} .
\]

\paragraph{Ratio-of-sums Spearman.}
When the relevant \(R_{jk}\)'s have compatible sign, the ratio-of-sums rule
\eqref{eq:ratio-sums} is the same linear estimating equation with row weights
proportional to \(R_{jk}^{-1}\):
\[
  \hat\gamma_i^{\mathrm{RS}}
  =
  \frac{\sum_{(j,k)\in\mathcal P_i}R_{ij}R_{ik}}
       {\sum_{(j,k)\in\mathcal P_i}R_{jk}} .
\]
This form is consistent whenever the pooled denominator has a nonzero
population limit, but it is not always a positive least-squares metric if the
within-block covariances have mixed signs.

\section{One-step GMM weights for the triad moments}

The itemwise construction can be stacked. Let \(\gamma\) collect the
correlation-scale communality proportions for all indicators. For every
admissible row \((i;j,k)\), write
\[
  g_{i;jk}(\gamma;R)
  =
  \gamma_iR_{jk}-R_{ij}R_{ik}.
\]
Stacking rows gives
\[
  g(\gamma;R)=A(R)\gamma-b(R),
\]
where each row of \(A(R)\) has a single nonzero entry \(R_{jk}\) in column
\(i\), and the corresponding row of \(b(R)\) is \(R_{ij}R_{ik}\). For any
positive semidefinite weight \(W\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stacked-gmm}
  \hat\gamma_W
  =
  \{A(R)\T W A(R)\}^{+}
  A(R)\T W b(R).
\end{equation}
This is a closed-form GMM estimator for the overidentified linear triad
moments. The identity choice \(W=I\) gives a stable baseline. A one-step
efficient version uses a preliminary consistent \(\tilde\gamma\), forms
\[
  \widehat\Omega_g
  =
  \widehat{\operatorname{AVar}}\{
    \sqrt n\,g(\tilde\gamma;R)
  \},
\]
and sets \(W=\widehat\Omega_g^{+}\) in \eqref{eq:stacked-gmm}. The covariance
\(\widehat\Omega_g\) is obtained by delta method from the asymptotic covariance
of the sample correlations; under normality this has a closed form, and under
nonnormality it can be estimated by a sandwich covariance of the correlation
vector.

\begin{remark}
The one-step estimator is efficient only within the declared triad-moment GMM
family. It is not a claim that the resulting full Guttman estimator is globally
efficient for CFA. The weight also affects finite-sample behavior and the
off-model diagonal-completion target.
\end{remark}

This gives a recommendable hierarchy. The average-of-ratios and ratio-of-sums
rules are historically recognizable certified rules. The identity linear-moment
rule is the simplest non-ratio method-of-moments default. The one-step GMM rule
is the natural efficiency-oriented member of the same closed-form family,
conditional on a chosen covariance model for the sample correlations.

\section{Normal-theory plug-in GLS weights}

The most convenient covariance model is the normal-theory covariance of the
sample correlations. This gives the direct analogue of SEM GLS for the triad
equations: keep the triad moments linear in \(\gamma\), but weight them by the
normal-theory sampling covariance implied by the current correlation matrix.

Let \(r\) collect the distinct off-diagonal entries of \(R\). Under
multivariate normality,
\[
  \Gamma_r^{\mathrm{NT}}(R)
  =
  \operatorname{AVar}\{\sqrt n(\hat r-r)\}
\]
has entries, for pairs \(u<v\) and \(w<z\),
\begin{align}\label{eq:nt-correlation-gamma}
  \Gamma^{\mathrm{NT}}_{uv,wz}(R)
  ={}&
  \rho_{uw}\rho_{vz}+\rho_{uz}\rho_{vw} \notag\\
  &-\rho_{uv}\{\rho_{uw}\rho_{uz}+\rho_{vw}\rho_{vz}\}
   -\rho_{wz}\{\rho_{uw}\rho_{vw}+\rho_{uz}\rho_{vz}\} \notag\\
  &+\frac{1}{2}\rho_{uv}\rho_{wz}
    \{\rho_{uw}^2+\rho_{uz}^2+\rho_{vw}^2+\rho_{vz}^2\},
\end{align}
with \(\rho_{aa}=1\). In particular,
\(\Gamma^{\mathrm{NT}}_{uv,uv}=(1-\rho_{uv}^2)^2\).

For a triad row \(g_{i;jk}(\gamma;R)=\gamma_iR_{jk}-R_{ij}R_{ik}\), the
Jacobian with respect to \(r\) has only three nonzero entries:
\[
  \frac{\partial g_{i;jk}}{\partial R_{jk}}=\gamma_i,
  \qquad
  \frac{\partial g_{i;jk}}{\partial R_{ij}}=-R_{ik},
  \qquad
  \frac{\partial g_{i;jk}}{\partial R_{ik}}=-R_{ij}.
\]
Let this sparse Jacobian be \(M(\gamma;R)\). Given a preliminary consistent
\(\tilde\gamma\), usually ILM, define
\[
  \widehat\Omega_{\mathrm{NT}}
  =
  M(\tilde\gamma;\hat R)
  \Gamma_r^{\mathrm{NT}}(\hat R)
  M(\tilde\gamma;\hat R)\T .
\]
The normal-theory GLS triad estimator is then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nt-gls-triad}
  \hat\gamma_{\mathrm{NT\text{-}GLS}}
  =
  \{A(\hat R)\T\widehat\Omega_{\mathrm{NT}}^{+}A(\hat R)\}^{+}
  A(\hat R)\T\widehat\Omega_{\mathrm{NT}}^{+}b(\hat R).
\end{equation}
The covariance-scale communality estimate is
\(\hat c_i=S_{ii}\hat\gamma_{\mathrm{NT\text{-}GLS},i}\).

If the observations are normal and the block model is correctly specified, this
plug-in weight converges to the optimal one-step triad-GMM weight. If the data
are nonnormal, or if the normal covariance is only used as a working model, the
same estimator remains a certified weighted triad rule as long as the limiting
weight is finite and nondegenerate, but the efficiency interpretation changes.

This is ``free'' in the statistical sense that no empirical fourth-moment
matrix, bootstrap, or CFA iteration is needed once \(S\) has been computed.
It is not computationally identical to ILM: one must build
\(\Gamma_r^{\mathrm{NT}}\), push it through the sparse triad Jacobian, and take
a generalized inverse or solve the corresponding GLS system. For a block with
\(m\) indicators this involves \(d=m(m-1)/2\) correlations and
\(t=m(m-1)(m-2)/2\) triad rows. In ordinary CFA block sizes this is small
relative to iterative NTML, but for large blocks it can dominate the
communality step.

There are two natural scopes. The blockwise version computes
\(\widehat\Omega_{\mathrm{NT}}\) separately inside each factor block. It is
simple, preserves the blockwise Guttman construction, and avoids using
cross-factor correlations to choose within-factor communalities. A full stacked
version forms one covariance matrix for all within-block triad rows. Under
correlated factors, the normal-theory covariance between sample correlations in
different blocks is generally nonzero, so the full weight can couple the
finite-sample estimates across blocks. This remains consistent because all
within-block triad moments are exact on the model, but under misspecification it
defines a different off-model diagonal-completion target. For that reason the
blockwise NT-GLS rule is the cleaner closed-form default, while the full stacked
rule should be reported as a distinct weighted variant.

\section{Relation to ADF efficiency on the correlation scale}

``Correlation scale'' means that the observed variables have first been
standardized. If
\[
  D_\Sigma=\diag(\Sigma),
  \qquad
  R=D_\Sigma^{-1/2}\Sigma D_\Sigma^{-1/2},
\]
then \(R\) is the population correlation matrix, with entries
\(\rho_{ij}\). The communality target on this scale is the communality
proportion
\[
  \gamma_i=\frac{c_i}{\sigma_{ii}}.
\]
The covariance-scale target is recovered by \(c_i=\sigma_{ii}\gamma_i\).
Working on the correlation scale removes arbitrary measurement units before
forming the triad equations.

Let \(r\) collect the off-diagonal entries of \(R\), and let
\[
  \Gamma_r
  =
  \operatorname{AVar}\{\sqrt n(\hat r-r_0)\}
\]
be the ADF covariance matrix for the sample correlations. If starting from the
usual covariance ADF matrix
\[
  \Gamma_s=\operatorname{AVar}\{\sqrt n(\hat s-s_0)\},
\]
then
\[
  \Gamma_r=J_r\Gamma_sJ_r\T,
  \qquad
  J_r=\frac{\partial r}{\partial s\T}.
\]
Thus the GMM weight for triad moments is a reduced ADF weight, but it is not
generally a principal submatrix. It is the covariance matrix of the nonlinear
triad moments:
\[
  \Omega_g
  =
  \operatorname{AVar}\{\sqrt n\,g(\gamma_0;\hat r)\}
  =
  M\Gamma_rM\T,
  \qquad
  M=\frac{\partial g(\gamma_0;r_0)}{\partial r\T}.
\]

For a one-factor correlation block, assume an orientation has been chosen and
write
\[
  \rho_{ij}=\alpha_i\alpha_j,
  \qquad
  \gamma_i=\alpha_i^2 .
\]
Let \(\mu(\gamma)\) be the oriented off-diagonal correlation model
\[
  \mu_{ij}(\gamma)=s_is_j\sqrt{\gamma_i\gamma_j},
  \qquad
  s_i=\operatorname{sign}(\alpha_i),
\]
and let
\[
  D=\frac{\partial \mu(\gamma_0)}{\partial \gamma\T}.
\]
The full off-diagonal ADF covariance for \(\gamma\) is
\[
  V_{\mathrm{ADF}}
  =
  (D\T\Gamma_r^{-1}D)^{-1}.
\]
The optimally weighted triad GMM covariance is
\[
  V_{\mathrm{triad}}
  =
  (G\T\Omega_g^{+}G)^{-1},
  \qquad
  G=\frac{\partial g(\gamma_0;r_0)}{\partial \gamma\T}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Optimal triad GMM and off-diagonal ADF]
\label{prop:triad-adf}
In a regular one-factor correlation block, suppose \(\Gamma_r\) is positive
definite, \(D\) has full column rank, and the triad Jacobian \(M\) has full
column rank as a map from the off-diagonal correlations to the stacked triad
moments. Then
\[
  V_{\mathrm{triad}}=V_{\mathrm{ADF}} .
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On the model, \(g\{\gamma,\mu(\gamma)\}=0\). Differentiating with respect to
\(\gamma\) gives
\[
  G+MD=0,
  \qquad
  G=-MD .
\]
Since \(M\) has full column rank and \(\Gamma_r\) is positive definite,
\[
  M\T(M\Gamma_rM\T)^{+}M=\Gamma_r^{-1}.
\]
Therefore
\[
  G\T\Omega_g^{+}G
  =
  D\T M\T(M\Gamma_rM\T)^{+}M D
  =
  D\T\Gamma_r^{-1}D .
\]
Taking inverses gives the result.
\end{proof}

Thus the conservative phrase ``efficient within the triad-moment GMM family''
can be sharpened in this simple case. For a regular one-factor block, optimal
triad GMM is first-order equivalent to the full ADF estimator that fits the
off-diagonal correlation model. The triad weight is the ADF correlation weight
transported through the triad-moment map.

This statement is still weaker than full CFA efficiency. It uses only the
off-diagonal correlation model for the block. It does not use the covariance
diagonal, the residual variances as model parameters, cross-factor covariance
structure, positivity constraints, or normal-theory likelihood structure.

\section{Two-factor correlation model}

For two correlated factors, the correlation-scale notation separates the
within-block and cross-block information. Let \(I_1\) and \(I_2\) be the two
indicator blocks. For \(i,j\in I_1\), \(a,b\in I_2\), write
\[
  \rho_{ij}=\alpha_i\alpha_j,
  \qquad
  \rho_{ab}=\beta_a\beta_b,
  \qquad
  \rho_{ia}=\kappa\alpha_i\beta_a ,
\]
where \(\kappa\) is the latent correlation. The communality proportions are
\[
  \gamma_i=\alpha_i^2,
  \qquad
  \delta_a=\beta_a^2 .
\]

The within-block triad moments remain exact:
\[
  \gamma_i\rho_{jk}-\rho_{ij}\rho_{ik}=0
  \qquad(i,j,k\in I_1),
\]
and
\[
  \delta_a\rho_{bc}-\rho_{ab}\rho_{ac}=0
  \qquad(a,b,c\in I_2).
\]
These are enough to recover the diagonal common variances block by block. They
ignore the cross-block correlations.

The full two-factor off-diagonal ADF model also uses
\[
  \rho_{ia}=\kappa\alpha_i\beta_a .
\]
Those cross-block entries contain information about \(\alpha\), \(\beta\), and
\(\kappa\) jointly. Consequently, the blockwise triad estimator is generally
ADF-equivalent only to the blockwise one-factor off-diagonal submodels, not to
the full two-factor off-diagonal model. Full ADF can be more efficient for the
communalities when \(\kappa\ne0\), because cross-block correlations provide
additional noisy measurements involving the same loadings.

The most useful additional moments keep one anchor in the target item's own
block. For \(i,j\in I_1\) and \(a\in I_2\),
\[
  \gamma_i\rho_{ja}-\rho_{ij}\rho_{ia}=0,
\]
because both sides are
\(\kappa\alpha_i^2\alpha_j\beta_a\). Similarly, for \(a,b\in I_2\) and
\(i\in I_1\),
\[
  \delta_a\rho_{ib}-\rho_{ai}\rho_{ab}=0 .
\]
These are cross-block analogues of the Spearman triads, but they remain linear
in the target communality. They add finite-sample information without adding
\(\kappa\) to the itemwise solve.

Other cross-block rank-one restrictions can also be written as moment
equations. For example,
\[
  \rho_{ia}\rho_{jb}
  =
  \kappa^2\rho_{ij}\rho_{ab}
  \qquad
  (i,j\in I_1,\ a,b\in I_2),
\]
so
\[
  \kappa^2\rho_{ij}\rho_{ab}-\rho_{ia}\rho_{jb}=0 .
\]
Also,
\[
  \rho_{ia}\rho_{ib}
  =
  \kappa^2\gamma_i\rho_{ab}.
\]
These equations show the difference between two extensions. One-same-block
anchor moments remain affine in the communalities. Moments whose two anchors
both lie outside the target block usually introduce latent-correlation nuisance
parameters such as \(\kappa^2\), and are no longer the same simple itemwise
linear solve unless an additional parameterization or one-step linearization is
chosen.

\section{Extended anchor-triad GMM}

The preceding two-factor identity has a direct many-factor form. Let
\[
  \Sigma=\Lambda\Phi\Lambda\T+\Theta
\]
be an independent-clusters CFA population with diagonal \(\Theta\). For
\(i\in I_f\), define
\[
  c_i=H_{ii}=\lambda_i^2\Phi_{ff}.
\]
Let \(j\in I_f\setminus\{i\}\), and let \(k\notin\{i,j\}\) be any other
indicator, possibly in another block \(I_g\). Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:anchor-triad}
  c_i\sigma_{jk}-\sigma_{ij}\sigma_{ik}=0 .
\end{equation}
Indeed,
\[
  c_i\sigma_{jk}
  =
  \lambda_i^2\Phi_{ff}\lambda_j\lambda_k\Phi_{fg}
  =
  \sigma_{ij}\sigma_{ik}.
\]
When \(g=f\), this is the ordinary within-block Spearman triad. When \(g\ne f\),
it uses a cross-block covariance but still estimates the same \(c_i\). If
\(\Phi_{fg}=0\), the row is exact but has zero first-order sensitivity to
\(c_i\), so it contributes no population information about \(c_i\) except
possibly as a control variate through its sampling covariance with other rows.

Choose any collection \(\mathcal T\) of anchor triples
\[
  r=(i;j,k),
  \qquad
  j\in I_{f(i)}\setminus\{i\},
  \qquad
  k\notin\{i,j\}.
\]
On the covariance scale, define
\[
  A_{r\ell}(\Sigma)
  =
  \begin{cases}
    \sigma_{jk}, & \ell=i,\\
    0,           & \ell\ne i,
  \end{cases}
  \qquad
  b_r(\Sigma)=\sigma_{ij}\sigma_{ik}.
\]
Then the population moment equations are simply
\[
  A(\Sigma)c-b(\Sigma)=0 .
\]
For a sample covariance \(S\) and a fixed positive semidefinite weight \(W\),
the weighted estimator remains closed form:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:extended-anchor-solve}
  \hat c_W
  =
  \{A(S)\T W A(S)\}^{+}A(S)\T W b(S).
\end{equation}
With a block-diagonal or diagonal \(W\), this is just a weighted itemwise
regression through the origin. With a full \(W\), different item equations can
be coupled, but the normal equations are still linear in \(c\).

Pure row-proportionality tetrads can be added without losing closed form. If
\(i\) and \(j\) are distinct indicators in the same block and \(k,l\) are any
other distinct indicators not equal to \(i\) or \(j\), then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pure-row-tetrad}
  \sigma_{ik}\sigma_{jl}-\sigma_{il}\sigma_{jk}=0 .
\end{equation}
These moments contain no \(c\), but under a full optimal GMM weight they can act
as control variates for the affine anchor-triad moments. Stack
\[
  m(c;S)=
  \begin{bmatrix}
    A(S)c-b(S)\\
    q(S)
  \end{bmatrix}
  =
  M(S)c+e(S),
\]
where \(q(S)\) stacks chosen pure tetrads and
\[
  M(S)=
  \begin{bmatrix}
    A(S)\\
    0
  \end{bmatrix},
  \qquad
  e(S)=
  \begin{bmatrix}
    -b(S)\\
    q(S)
  \end{bmatrix}.
\]
For fixed \(W\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affine-gmm-solve}
  \hat c_W
  =
  -\{M(S)\T W M(S)\}^{+}M(S)\T W e(S).
\end{equation}
Thus adding the rank restrictions changes the rows and the weight, not the
closed-form character of the communality solve.

The efficiency statement is the usual GMM one. If \(W\) converges to the
generalized inverse of the asymptotic covariance of the stacked moments, then
\eqref{eq:affine-gmm-solve} is efficient within the declared affine moment
family. If the chosen anchor triads and pure tetrads form a locally complete set
of off-diagonal independent-clusters restrictions, their optimally weighted GMM
estimator is first-order equivalent to the corresponding off-diagonal
minimum-distance estimator for \(c\). Under normality, the convenient plug-in
choice is the normal-theory covariance of the sample covariance or correlation
matrix pushed through the Jacobian of \(m(c;S)\), with \(c\) replaced by any
regular preliminary estimate. This gives a one-step efficient version while
keeping the final update linear.

There is still a boundary. If both anchors lie outside the target block, then
\[
  \sigma_{ij}\sigma_{ik}
  =
  c_i
  \frac{\Phi_{fg}\Phi_{fh}}{\Phi_{ff}\Phi_{gh}}
  \sigma_{jk},
  \qquad j\in I_g,\ k\in I_h ,
\]
when the displayed factor covariances are nonzero. Such rows are exact, but
they introduce latent-correlation nuisance parameters. They belong to a larger
nonlinear or one-step-linearized GMM system, not to the purely affine
communality solve above.

\section{Many-factor consistency and orthogonal-block efficiency}

Let the indicators be partitioned into simple-structure blocks
\[
  I_1,\ldots,I_q .
\]
On the correlation scale, write
\[
  \rho_{ij}=\alpha_i\alpha_j
  \qquad(i,j\in I_f,\ i\ne j),
\]
and let
\[
  \gamma_i=\alpha_i^2 .
\]
For each block \(I_f\), stack the within-block triad moments
\[
  g_{f,i;jk}(\gamma;R)
  =
  \gamma_iR_{jk}-R_{ij}R_{ik},
  \qquad
  i,j,k\in I_f,\quad i,j,k\hbox{ distinct}.
\]
Let \(g_{\mathrm{blk}}\) be the stack of these moments over all factors. Let
\[
  r_{\mathrm{blk}}
\]
collect the within-block off-diagonal correlations, and let
\[
  r_{\mathrm{cross}}
\]
collect the cross-block correlations. The full off-diagonal correlation vector
is \(r=(r_{\mathrm{blk}}\T,r_{\mathrm{cross}}\T)\T\).

\begin{proposition}[Blockwise triad consistency and efficiency]
\label{prop:blockwise-triad-efficiency}
Assume a regular simple-structure CFA correlation model with diagonal residual
covariance, at least three indicators per factor, and nonzero within-block
loadings. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The within-block triad moments are exact at the population model.
  Hence any regular blockwise weighted triad estimator is consistent for
  \(\gamma_i=c_i/\sigma_{ii}\), even when the latent factors are correlated.
\item Suppose, in addition, that the latent factors are orthogonal, so the
  population cross-block correlations are zero, and that the ADF covariance of
  sample correlations separates the within-block and cross-block parts:
  \[
    \Gamma_r
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
      \Gamma_{\mathrm{blk}} & 0\\
      0 & \Gamma_{\mathrm{cross}}
    \end{bmatrix}.
  \]
  Then the optimally weighted blockwise triad GMM estimator, with
  \[
    W=\Omega_{\mathrm{blk}}^{+},
    \qquad
    \Omega_{\mathrm{blk}}
    =
    M_{\mathrm{blk}}\Gamma_{\mathrm{blk}}M_{\mathrm{blk}}\T,
  \]
  is first-order equivalent to the full off-diagonal ADF estimator for
  \(\gamma\). In particular,
  \[
    V_{\mathrm{triad}}(\gamma)=V_{\mathrm{ADF}}(\gamma).
  \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For item \(i\in I_f\), the within-block correlation model gives
\[
  \rho_{ij}\rho_{ik}
  =
  \alpha_i^2 \rho_{jk}
  =
  \gamma_i\rho_{jk}
\]
at the population point, for every distinct \(j,k\in I_f\). This proves exact
moments and consistency by the same argument as Proposition
\ref{prop:linear-mom}.

For the efficiency statement, orthogonal factors imply that
\[
  \rho_{ia}=0\qquad(i\in I_f,\ a\in I_g,\ f\ne g).
\]
The first derivative of these cross-block correlations with respect to
\(\gamma\) is zero at the orthogonal point; cross-block correlations only
identify latent correlations to first order. Therefore the ADF Jacobian for
\(\gamma\) lives entirely in the within-block part,
\[
  D=
  \begin{bmatrix}
    D_{\mathrm{blk}}\\
    0
  \end{bmatrix}.
\]
With the displayed block-separating \(\Gamma_r\),
\[
  D\T\Gamma_r^{-1}D
  =
  D_{\mathrm{blk}}\T\Gamma_{\mathrm{blk}}^{-1}D_{\mathrm{blk}} .
\]
Within each factor block, Proposition \ref{prop:triad-adf} applies. Stacking
the blocks gives
\[
  G_{\mathrm{blk}}\T\Omega_{\mathrm{blk}}^{+}G_{\mathrm{blk}}
  =
  D_{\mathrm{blk}}\T\Gamma_{\mathrm{blk}}^{-1}D_{\mathrm{blk}} .
\]
The inverse of this common information matrix is both
\(V_{\mathrm{triad}}(\gamma)\) and \(V_{\mathrm{ADF}}(\gamma)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The block-separating ADF covariance condition is automatic under multivariate
normality when the factor covariance matrix is diagonal: the observed factor
blocks are then independent. Under arbitrary nonnormality, zero latent
covariance does not by itself rule out fourth-moment dependence across blocks.
Full ADF may then use cross-block sample correlations as control-variate
information. The blockwise triad estimator remains consistent, but the
efficiency equality above need not hold.
\end{remark}

\section{Can the \(H\) step be block-equivariant?}

Let \(L=\bigoplus_f L_f\) be a nonsingular linear transformation that acts
inside the declared indicator blocks. A fully block-equivariant \(H\)-functional
would satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-equiv-H}
  \mathcal H(L\Sigma L\T)=L\,\mathcal H(\Sigma)\,L\T .
\end{equation}
This is the right algebraic target if the observed variables inside a factor
block are treated as coordinates rather than named indicators. It is stronger
than the invariance used by the triad rules above.

The difficulty is structural. The simple CFA model used by the triad identities
is not closed under general \(L_f\)'s. If
\[
  \Sigma=\Lambda\Phi\Lambda\T+\Theta,
  \qquad \Theta \hbox{ diagonal},
\]
then
\[
  L\Sigma L\T
  =
  (L\Lambda)\Phi(L\Lambda)\T+L\Theta L\T,
\]
and \(L\Theta L\T\) is generally dense within each indicator block. Hence the
transformed covariance no longer has the property that within-block
off-diagonal covariances are pure common covariances. The Spearman triads and
their weighted GMM variants are therefore tied to the named-indicator
coordinates in which the uniqueness matrix is diagonal.

\begin{proposition}[No universal block-equivariant \(H\) rule]
\label{prop:no-block-equiv-H}
For a single factor block with \(m\ge3\), there is no covariance functional
\(\mathcal H\) that is both
\begin{enumerate}
\item exact on every regular one-factor diagonal-uniqueness population
  \[
    \Sigma=\lambda\lambda\T+\Theta,
    \qquad \Theta \hbox{ positive diagonal},
  \]
  in the sense that \(\mathcal H(\Sigma)=\lambda\lambda\T\), and
\item equivariant under all nonsingular within-block transformations, in the
  sense of \eqref{eq:block-equiv-H}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let \(u=m^{-1/2}\mathbf 1\), and for any \(a>0\) consider the regular
one-factor population
\[
  \Sigma_a=I+a^2uu\T,
  \qquad
  H_a=a^2uu\T .
\]
This is a diagonal-uniqueness model with \(\Theta=I\) and \(\lambda=au\).
If \(\mathcal H\) were exact and block-equivariant, applying
\(L_a=\Sigma_a^{-1/2}\) would give
\[
  \mathcal H(I)
  =
  L_aH_aL_a\T
  =
  \frac{a^2}{1+a^2}uu\T .
\]
The right-hand side depends on \(a\). The same covariance input \(I\) would
therefore require different outputs for different diagonal-uniqueness
populations. This contradicts that \(\mathcal H\) is a single-valued
covariance functional.
\end{proof}

The proposition is deliberately about the \(H\) step, not the later Guttman
reconstruction map. It says that no reweighting of the within-block triad
moments can produce a generally valid block-equivariant estimator for the same
diagonal-residual CFA target. The obstruction already appears in one block, and
therefore also appears in many-factor models whenever a factor block has no
usable cross-block information, as with orthogonal latent factors.

\paragraph{What remains possible.}
Several weaker or more conditional equivariance statements are still useful.
First, the triad family is compatible with transformations that preserve
diagonal uniqueness, such as relabeling, sign changes, and diagonal rescaling
of named indicators. The correlation-scale implementation mainly packages this
ordinary scale equivariance.

Second, after a candidate \(H\) has been supplied, the Guttman reconstruction
map can be made equivariant by moving the composite-score rule with the loading
coordinates. This is the loading-aligned construction in the criterion note.
That property is conditional on having an \(H\) that already transforms as in
\eqref{eq:block-equiv-H}.

Third, one can define a different, cross-block construction under stronger
assumptions. If residual covariances are allowed to be unrestricted inside each
indicator block, then the model class is closed under block transformations:
\[
  \Sigma_{ff}=H_{ff}+\Psi_{ff},\qquad
  \Sigma_{fg}=H_{fg}\quad(f\ne g),
\]
with unrestricted positive definite \(\Psi_{ff}\). The cross-block matrices
\(\Sigma_{fg}\) are then residual-free and transform equivariantly. After
block whitening, their leading singular vectors can identify loading
directions when the factor-correlation graph is connected and the relevant
rank-one singular values are separated. But the norms of the block loading
vectors and the latent correlations remain confounded without an additional
normalization or structural assumption; when factors are orthogonal, the
cross-block matrices are zero and provide no information at all. This is a
different estimator family, not a block-equivariant version of the
within-block triad communality rule.

\section{Uniqueness-first rules}

The paper also points to uniqueness estimators \cite{iharakano1986,cudeck1991}.
For the Guttman estimator, the relevant population object is not the uniqueness
itself but
\[
  h_i=\sigma_{ii}-u_i .
\]
A uniqueness-first rule is acceptable only if it is exact on the population
factor model.

\begin{proposition}[Exact uniqueness implies exact communality]
\label{prop:uniqueness}
Suppose a population functional \(u(\Sigma)\in\mathbb R^p\) is defined on a
class of identified factor models and satisfies
\[
  u(\Sigma_0)=\diag(\Theta_0)
\]
whenever
\[
  \Sigma_0=\Lambda_0\Phi_0\Lambda_0\T+\Theta_0
\]
with diagonal \(\Theta_0\). Then
\[
  h_i^u(\Sigma)=\sigma_{ii}-u_i(\Sigma)
\]
satisfies \(h_i^u(\Sigma_0)=e_i\T\Lambda_0\Phi_0\Lambda_0\T e_i\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At \(\Sigma_0\),
\[
  \sigma_{0,ii}
  =
  e_i\T\Lambda_0\Phi_0\Lambda_0\T e_i+\Theta_{0,ii}.
\]
Subtracting \(u_i(\Sigma_0)=\Theta_{0,ii}\) gives the claim.
\end{proof}

One exact population reading is the rank-\(k\) uniqueness problem: find a
diagonal \(\Theta\) such that \(\Sigma-\Theta\) is positive semidefinite, has
rank \(k\), and satisfies the required loading-pattern restrictions. If the
solution is unique at \(\Sigma_0\), then Proposition~\ref{prop:uniqueness}
applies. This is the standard needed for treating a uniqueness estimator as a
Guttman communality rule. A numerical algorithm may approximate this target, but
the target itself must be exact on the model manifold.

\section{What not to certify}

Several common initial communality guesses are useful starts but are not
consistent estimators of \(c_i\) for a finite congeneric factor block.

\paragraph{Squared multiple correlation.}
For a covariance matrix \(\Sigma\), the SMC communality is
\[
  h_i^{\mathrm{SMC}}
  =
  \sigma_{ii}-\{(\Sigma^{-1})_{ii}\}^{-1}.
\]
In a one-factor block, write
\[
  \Sigma=\Theta+\phi\lambda\lambda\T,\qquad
  t_i=\frac{\phi\lambda_i^2}{\theta_i},\qquad
  \delta=\phi\sum_m\frac{\lambda_m^2}{\theta_m}.
\]
The Sherman-Morrison formula gives
\[
  \{(\Sigma^{-1})_{ii}\}^{-1}
  =
  \theta_i\frac{1+\delta}{1+\delta-t_i},
\]
and hence
\[
  h_i^{\mathrm{SMC}}
  =
  c_i\frac{\delta-t_i}{1+\delta-t_i}.
\]
This is strictly below \(c_i\) at finite block size when \(c_i>0\) and the
other indicators carry finite information. It approaches \(c_i\) only in a
large-block or near-perfect-measurement limit. Therefore SMC is not an exact
population communality rule for the Guttman estimator.

\paragraph{Bounds and repairs.}
Clamps to \([0,\sigma_{ii}]\), wide lavaan bounds, positive-definite repairs, and
ridge corrections can make a finite-sample procedure safer. They are not
population communality estimators unless the repair is inactive at the target.
If a repair is active at \(\Sigma_0\), the estimator has changed target.

\section{Implementation implications}

For a pure Guttman estimator, \texttt{magmaan} should distinguish
communality rules by their population contract:
\begin{itemize}
\item certified rules: single triads, average-of-ratios Spearman, ratio-of-sums
  Spearman, identity linear moments, weighted linear-moment variants with
  nonzero denominators, one-step triad GMM with a declared correlation
  covariance model, the normal-theory plug-in GLS weight
  \eqref{eq:nt-gls-triad}, extended anchor-triad GMM
  \eqref{eq:extended-anchor-solve}, and exact uniqueness-first rules;
\item start-value rules only: SMC, arbitrary lower bounds, and repairs that can
  be active at the population target;
\item regularized variants: any rule that replaces a failed denominator or
  singular uniqueness problem by a ridge or projection should be labeled as a
  regularized Guttman variant.
\end{itemize}

The current frontier Guttman estimator uses the average-of-ratios
Spearman-Harman rule through the shared \texttt{theta\_spearman()} helper, and
then subtracts the resulting residual variance from the diagonal. On an exact
simple CFA block this is population-consistent for
\(\diag(\Lambda\Phi\Lambda\T)\). If we add alternatives, the test for admission
should be the propositions above, not historical popularity as a starting value.
For a recommendable closed-form default, the identity linear-moment rule
\eqref{eq:identity-linear-mom} is the least ratio-sensitive member of the
certified family. For an efficiency-oriented normal-theory variant, use the
blockwise NT-GLS rule \eqref{eq:nt-gls-triad}, or the extended anchor-triad
version \eqref{eq:extended-anchor-solve} when correlated factors make
cross-block anchor rows informative. More general one-step GMM rules
\eqref{eq:stacked-gmm} should declare the covariance model used to construct
the weight and the moment rows included.
