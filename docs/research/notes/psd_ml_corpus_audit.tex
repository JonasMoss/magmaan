\section*{Decision summary}

Keep ordinary normal-theory maximum likelihood (NTML) as the core,
lavaan-compatible estimator.  Retain the post-fit covariance audit and expose
positive-semidefinite ML (PSD-ML) as an explicit frontier refit.  Do not make
direct PSD-ML or automatic refitting the default.  Promote a small,
geometry-diverse subset of the corpus findings to deterministic regression
tests; do not place the full corpus scan in continuous integration.

This decision follows from a 97-case audit.  Ordinary NTML was
covariance-admissible in 91 cases.  In those cases, PSD-ML changed no parameter
by more than \(3.21\times 10^{-6}\), and the maximum absolute objective change
was \(7.18\times 10^{-12}\).  Six ordinary fits were inadmissible.  All 97
PSD-ML refits converged and were admissible.

\section*{Scope}

The audit used the checked-in continuous model and summary-statistic fixtures
for Little, Newsom, Geiser, the retained Mplus tranche, the Kline--Guo
invariance cases, and the first paper-corpus case.  The private raw-data corpus
was not required.  Each case was re-estimated by ordinary complete-data NTML,
audited for positive semidefiniteness of the primitive LISREL covariance
matrices \(\Theta\) and \(\Psi\), and warm-refitted by PSD-ML.

The Geiser files are GLS/ULS oracle fixtures.  Their model and data summaries
were deliberately re-estimated by NTML for this question.  The audit therefore
describes the NTML behavior of those model--data combinations, not the
published Geiser estimator.

\section*{Differing cases}

Let \(\Delta f=f_{\mathrm{PSD}}-f_{\mathrm{ordinary}}\).  The last column below
puts the objective change on the ordinary likelihood-ratio scale.

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{llllr}
\toprule
Source & Case & Failing block & Minimum eigenvalue & \(2N\Delta f\) \\
\midrule
Little & two-indicator CFA       & \(\Theta\) & \(-0.001186\) & \(0.0006\) \\
Little & four-wave model 3a      & \(\Psi\)   & \(-0.011344\) & \(0\) \\
Little & four-wave model 3b      & \(\Psi\)   & \(-0.017695\) & \(0\) \\
Little & linear growth model 4c  & \(\Psi\)   & \(-0.020951\) & \(27.41\) \\
Geiser & second-order CFA        & \(\Psi\)   & \(-0.098807\) & \(0.0067\) \\
Geiser & quadratic growth        & \(\Psi\)   & \(-0.000476\) & \(0.914\) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Classical boundary repair.}
The Little two-indicator CFA estimated the residual variance of
\texttt{Glad} as \(-0.001186\).  PSD-ML put it at zero.  The Geiser
second-order CFA estimated the disturbance variance of \texttt{KFT\_N} as
\(-0.098807\); PSD-ML put it at \(1.15\times 10^{-7}\).  Both repairs had
negligible likelihood cost.

\paragraph{Same fit, different decomposition.}
In Little models 3a and 3b, the latent level--slope covariance implied a
correlation exceeding one.  The level factor loads only on the first wave
there, so its variance and the first-wave residual variance are not separately
identified by the observed covariance matrix.  PSD-ML transferred
approximately \(0.03175\) from the first-wave residual variance to the level
variance, reached the PSD boundary, and left the objective unchanged.

\paragraph{Materially different fit.}
Little model 4c estimated the slope variance as \(-0.02092\).  Under PSD-ML it
became \(0.00365\), the level variance moved from \(0.216\) to \(0.823\), and
the first-wave residual variance moved from \(0.475\) to zero.  The resulting
likelihood-ratio-scale cost was \(27.41\).  This is the clearest corpus example
in which covariance honesty changes both the substantive estimates and model
fit.

\paragraph{Joint covariance geometry.}
The Geiser quadratic-growth case had positive factor variances but an
indefinite joint growth-factor covariance matrix.  PSD-ML moved the solution
to the boundary with a likelihood-ratio-scale cost of \(0.914\).  This case
guards a feature that separate nonnegative-variance bounds do not express.

\section*{Action}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Keep the current ordinary-fit warning and structured audit.  It catches
the exact cases for which the admissible model differs.
\item Keep PSD-ML opt-in.  Automatic replacement would silently change the
estimand or choose among observationally equivalent decompositions, and
boundary inference is not yet regularized.
\item Treat ``ordinary fit, audit, explicit warm PSD refit'' as the recommended
methods-development workflow.  A convenience policy wrapper is useful only
when a concrete R consumer asks for it; it is not needed to validate the
estimator.
\item Promote four deterministic regression geometries: Little model 3a for
same-fit reallocation, Little model 4c for a material constrained optimum,
Geiser second-order CFA for a negative structural disturbance, and Geiser
quadratic growth for a joint covariance violation.  Prefer compact extracted
summary fixtures over running the complete corpus in the default suite.
\item Prioritize finite-difference derivative gates plus multi-group,
mean-structure, and equality-constraint coverage before expanding PSD-ML to
FIML or weighted least-squares estimators.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Reproduction}

The advisory runner is
\path{tests/checks/psd_ml_corpus/psd_ml_corpus_audit.R}.  It writes the
case-level audit, parameter changes, corpus counts, failures, and metadata
under its ignored \path{results/} directory.

\begin{verbatim}
just -f tests/checks/psd_ml_corpus/justfile audit
\end{verbatim}

The recorded run used magmaan 0.0.1 at source commit
\texttt{7e7f00d0}.  Ordinal, mixed, FIML, multilevel, and native
factor--composite estimators remain outside this audit because the current
covariance-honest estimator is complete-data NTML only.
